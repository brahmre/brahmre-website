Learn

What are woodland conservation credits?

Maryland's Forest Conservation Act integrates forest protection into development review. Depending on the project and reviewing jurisdiction, mitigation may include on-site or off-site measures, fee-in-lieu, or approved forest/woodland bank credits.

Terminology varies. Buyers may search for woodland conservation credits, forest conservation credits, forest mitigation bank credits, afforestation credits, preservation credits or woodland conservation certificates. BRAHM RE is designed to route those searches to the applicable credit and jurisdiction rather than make the buyer decode the terminology first.

Educational content should never promise that a particular credit will be accepted for a project until the applicable county/municipal or State reviewer confirms the requirement.
Why would a project need credits?

Development review can create a forest conservation obligation based on existing forest, clearing, land-use category, retention and afforestation/reforestation calculations. The project's approved plan or reviewing agency determines what must be satisfied.

Can credits be used anywhere in Maryland?

No blanket statewide assumption should be made. Acceptance depends on the governing program, bank approval, location and the project's specific mitigation requirement.

What's the difference between afforestation and reforestation?

Afforestation establishes forest where forest cover has been absent for a long period or on open areas not presently forested. Reforestation restores/replaces qualifying forest in response to applicable requirements.

Can I purchase before my project is approved?

The safest purchase flow is to establish the project's requirement and confirm the correct credit class and jurisdictional eligibility before funds are released.
